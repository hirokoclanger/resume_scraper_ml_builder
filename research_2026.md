## The Core Question

**Should I have one strong resume reflecting who I am, or tailor for
every role?**

Answer: **Tailor every time, even if you know your lane.** Not because
you don't know yourself — but because the ATS between you and the
recruiter doesn't care what you know about yourself. It cares whether
your resume mirrors the listing.

---

## Why Tailoring Wins in 2026

- **99.7 %** of recruiters use ATS filters (Jobscan State of the Job Search 2025)
- **98.4 %** of Fortune 500 companies use one
- **76.4 %** of recruiters filter by skills first
- **55.3 %** filter by job titles
- **50.6 %** filter by certifications
- **10.6×** — interview rate boost when your resume's job title matches the listing exactly
- **40 – 60 %** higher ATS scores for tailored vs. generic versions of the same resume

The old "ATS rejects 75 % of resumes" stat is outdated. The real risk
isn't rejection — it's **ranking**. You don't get filtered out; you get
buried under candidates who mirrored the listing better.

## The Practical Workflow (10–15 min per app)

### 1. Keep a master resume

Your full history, every accomplishment, every skill. This is the source
of truth — never sent as-is.

### 2. For each application, change only

- **Job title line** under your name → match the listing's exact title
- **Professional summary** (2–4 sentences) → rewrite around the role's keywords
- **2–3 bullets per role** → reframe to mirror the listing's language
- **Skills section** → reorder so the listing's keywords surface first

### 3. Keyword targets

- Match **70 – 80 %** of the listing's keywords
- Include **3 – 5 exact-match keywords** for the most critical skills
- Never keyword-stuff — keep language natural

### 4. Use AI for the boring layer, not the high-stakes layer

- ✅ AI for: extracting top skills / certs / title from the listing, rewording bullets to match
- ❌ AI for: writing accomplishments from scratch, fabricating metrics, full takeover

Every bullet should lead with a **specific, quantified, verifiable
accomplishment** no AI could invent. That's what survives the human
read after the filter.

## Format Rules That Still Matter

- **File type:** `.docx` parses most reliably across ATS systems in 2026
- **Length:** Signal density > page count. 1 page for 0 – 5 yrs
  experience; 2 pages fine for 5+ yrs
- **Avoid:** Tables, text boxes, graphics, columns, headers/footers
  with key info
- **Professional summary**, not objective statement
- **Action verbs** to start every bullet
- **ATS test:** Paste resume into plain `.txt` — if order/content
  survives, formatting is safe

## The Job Description Filtering Problem

Big boards (LinkedIn, Indeed) have unreliable filters: "remote" jobs
that aren't remote, fake salary ranges, "entry-level" requiring 5 years.

### What actually helps

- **Aggregators with better filters:** Simplify, Teal, JobRight
- **Niche boards** for your specific field instead of general boards
- **Specific title strings** in alerts ("Staff Backend Engineer" >
  "Software Engineer")
- **Direct company career pages** — pick 20 – 30 target companies,
  monitor those instead of relying on aggregators
- **Set Boolean searches** where supported (e.g., LinkedIn's
  `"title" AND "location" NOT "contract"`)

## Quick Decision Checklist (Before Hitting Apply)

- [ ] Does my resume's job title line match the listing's title?
- [ ] Are 3 – 5 exact keywords from the listing present in my resume?
- [ ] Have I rewritten the summary for this specific role?
- [ ] Have I adjusted 2 – 3 bullets to mirror the listing's language?
- [ ] Does every bullet have a number, percentage, or concrete outcome?
- [ ] Is the file a `.docx`?
- [ ] Did I run the plain-text test?

---

## Bottom Line

Knowing what you want and being experienced is necessary but not
sufficient. The system rewards **alignment**, not just **competence**.
Tailoring is the price of being seen at all.
